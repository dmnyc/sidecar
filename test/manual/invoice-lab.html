Sidecar invoice lab

The rule: if you asked for it, you get the overlay card; if you might not know it is there, you get the corner pill. "Asked" means the invoice is inside a payment panel you opened, which is decided by markup (a dialog role), by a QR beside it, or by layout (a fixed overlay).

Every invoice here is structurally valid and completely bogus: 21 sats, fake payment hash, no signature. Nothing can be spent, and a wallet will reject it if you do press Pay.

1. A lightning: link, just sitting there

The reporter's class of problem. Expect a pill, no card. Then click the link itself: that opens the card, because a tap is a request to pay.

2. An invoice beside a QR code

The shape of every zap modal, and a QR is something you had to open. Expect the card.

3. An invoice in a field inside a dialog

Expect the card. A field only counts when it sits in a dialog or beside a QR, and role="dialog" is the plainest way a page says so.

4. The page copies an invoice to your clipboard

A gesture, but with no payment panel on screen to tie it to, so it stays quiet. Expect a pill. Copying from inside a real modal takes case 2's path instead and stays loud.

5. A bare invoice in a paragraph

Expect nothing whatsoever. Invoice text on its own is not a pay intent, and if this ever produces a pill the detector has grown too broad.

6. An expired invoice as a link

Expect nothing. Expired invoices are filtered before anything is offered.

7. An invoice that carries a memo

Same as case 1, with a BOLT11 description on it. Expect a pill, and the memo on the card once you open it: the card has always shown one, there just wasn't an invoice around with a description to show.

8. A payment panel like a real client's

A fixed overlay with a QR image and a lightning: link inside, which is what Jumble's zap panel looks like. Expect the card. This is the case that was broken: the link branch runs first and used to stamp it passive before anything looked at the panel around it.

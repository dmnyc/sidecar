Sidecar's four new themes

The four new themes, each at least 75 years in the past: Ben Day and Underground for light, Departure and Jazz Age for dark. That takes the set from sixteen to twenty, ten of each. Arcade and Hi-Fi are parked at the bottom for later.

Every mockup shows the same sidebar content: the account bar with two icons, the view title, the balance, a note and a signing request. Manrope is the body face throughout, as in the extension. These are sketches; the colors have not been through theme-contrast.test.js.

Light · decided

Ben Day keeping

Light · Bangers display · slug ben-day
#FDFBF4 #E3262B #FFD400 #1A5FB4 #111111

Legibility fix: the dots are now the gutter between panels, never the ground under words. This is how comics are lettered anyway.

  • The header is a white panel with a 3px keyline under it. The name and icons never touch a dot.
  • Icon buttons are white squares with black outlines and a heavier 2.4 stroke.
  • The view title sits in its own white box. The balance stays in the yellow caption box.
  • The dots are lighter (55% opacity) because they no longer have to compete with type.
  • The POW! burst moved beside the balance, so it never covers a figure.

Names

Ben DayThe dot process itself. Two words, like Film Noir and Par Avion. My pick.
Pop ArtThe movement. Matches Bauhaus and Ukiyo-e, but it is broader than dots and outlines.
Four-ColorThe comic-book printing process. Accurate, but reads as a spec.
KapowFun, and it names the payment moment. No other theme is named for a sound.
Sunday FunniesWarm, but two long words on a gallery card.
Whaam!A specific Lichtenstein painting. Avoid, like the artist's own name: no other theme is named for a person or a single work.

Underground keeping

Light · mosaic lettering for titles and the balance, Pathway Gothic for enamel signs
tile #F3F0E7 tablet #141E26 border #6FA59A faience #7E2A22 frieze #D8C690 IND green #1F4A3B cobalt #1E2E78

Idea: the 1904 IRT stations and the enamel signs that followed. The field is white subway tile. The header is a mosaic name tablet in a border of glazed tile, like 34th Street. The balance is a red tablet with a gold fillet, like 23rd Street. A tesserae frieze runs along the top. Everything you press is a porcelain-enamel sign: IND green, cobalt, or white with the inset line and chipped edges.

Legibility: the header is the most legible in the set. It's a solid dark tablet with cream lettering, and the tile grid never sits behind words or icons. Notes and requests are white enamel plates.

Signature moment: when a payment lands, the balance's tesserae are set again one by one into the new figure. The mockup replays it every few seconds.

How the mosaic works: the title and figures are drawn at runtime from the real string, so translated titles and any balance render correctly. The real text stays in the DOM for screen readers and copying. Long German titles get smaller tesserae instead of being cut off.

Watch for: Brownstone is also New York. That's fine: Brownstone is the street at night, Underground is the station below it. The other risk is Ben Day: both are light themes with chunky lettering, but tile and enamel have nothing in common with dots and outlines.

Names: Underground reads well, but to many people it means the London Tube. Alternatives are Subway, the plain New York word; IRT, the 1904 line these stations belong to; and Tesserae, which names the tilework rather than the railway.

Dark · decided

Departure keeping

Dark · Barlow Condensed display
#1A1A19 #0E0E0D #F2F1EA #F5C400

Idea: a station departures board. Every character of the title and balance sits on its own flap tile, and the yellow is the platform number.

Sets it apart: the balance becomes a machine you can hear. The flip plays on every balance change.

Signature moment: digits clatter through to the new value, one tile after another.

Legibility: the account bar now sits on a solid board strip, so the name and icons never cross the empty flap grid. Title and balance characters each have their own solid tile.

Name: Departure, singular, reads as a theme name rather than a sign. The board itself would still say "Departures" if it ever appears as a label.

Era: Solari's split-flap board dates from 1956, just inside the 75-year line. The theme can lean on the older station indicator boards it replaced, which puts it squarely in the period.

Watch for: Nixie already makes the balance a display device. This one is mechanical where Nixie is electrical, and it is the closest overlap any of the four new themes has.

Jazz Age keeping

Dark · Fascinate title, Federo figures · 1920s
stage #0C0908 shade #B3122E ivory #F3E7CF bronze #B8864B glow #FF563C

Idea: the instruments and the stage, as in your references. The title is set in Fascinate, a free face drawn after Broadway (1928), the lettering of Jazz Age posters and programs.

  • Keyboard: a run of piano keys along the top edge, where Underground has its frieze.
  • Stage lighting: spotlight beams from lamps above the frame, one warm white, one red gel, one amber, landing in a soft pool on the stage floor at the bottom.
  • The balance is written on the stave: the figures stand across the five lines like notes, between an opening bar line and a closing double bar. A dark halo cuts the lines away round each figure, so they never run through a digit.

Signature moment: when a payment lands, the figures on the stave bounce one after another, as if the new balance was just played.

Speakeasy: this sits closest to it, since both are 1920s nights out. Speakeasy is the hidden bar, in purple velvet and gold. This is the bandstand: black, crimson and ivory, with no gold anywhere.

Buttons: lit like a club sign, with a hot core, a halo that spills onto the black and a soft reflection underneath. The primary button breathes slowly and flickers once, and stays still with reduced motion.

Deferred: a continuous-line drawing of instruments for the background. The instrument shapes worked; the line joining them did not. Parked until the idea is clearer.

Parked for later · outside the period

Arcade parked

Dark · Press Start 2P display
#07060D #FF3E9A #3DE0FF #FFE14D #52F27A

Idea: a 1981 cabinet. Pixel starfield, faint scanlines, sprite avatars and a high-score balance. It's the dark counterpart to Ben Day's pop culture.

Sets it apart: the only bitmap type in the set, and the only theme that's playful after dark.

Signature moment: an incoming zap pops a "+500" coin over the balance.

Legibility: the same rule as Ben Day. The scanlines now sit behind the content, not over the type, and the stars are dimmer and kept out of the header, so the account name and icons have plain black behind them.

Watch for: Press Start 2P is wide and heavy. It works for titles, figures and the account name, but never for body text or long labels, and German strings will run out of room fast.

Hi-Fi parked

Light · Michroma labels, Saira Semi Condensed counter figures
aluminum #CFCFCA walnut #4A2C1A LED #2FD35A drum #151515

Idea: a 1970s silver-face deck. The panel is brushed aluminum with a walnut cabinet edge, and every label is engraved in a wide face, including the bracketed view title. The balance is the roller-drum tape counter, with its units engraved under it.

No VU meter on the wallet. A meter needs something to measure, and a balance is a number, not a level. The counter already says the balance exactly, so the meter is gone.

Signature moment: when a payment arrives, the counter's drums roll up to the new figure. The mockup plays this every five seconds.

Legibility: dark engraving on light metal is high-contrast by nature, and the brushing is kept faint. The counter is white on black.

Name: with the design built around the equipment, Solid State now fits too; it was the badge on decks from exactly this era. Hi-Fi is shorter and friendlier on a gallery card.

Watch for:

  • Departure: it also changes digits mechanically. Departure's flaps clatter over one character at a time; this counter rolls smoothly. Shipping both means keeping that difference sharp.
  • Nixie: it's also a control panel. Nixie is cold dark steel with one hot red readout; this is warm, light, silver and walnut.
  • Mode: a silver-face deck makes this a light theme. Added outright, it's the 21st theme and the 11th light one.
